9:30 AM
Pine Bank Arch — where you got engaged📍 Directionsyour bridge
The cast-iron arch near 62nd Street on the West Side, just east of the West Drive by Heckscher Playground — one of Calvert Vaux's original ornamental cast-iron bridges from the 1860s, carrying the footpath over the old bridle trail. Take your time here; this is the morning's point. (The Conservancy lists a restoration of Pinebank — new decking, recast ironwork, fresh paint — as under construction, with no dates posted; glance at its alerts the week before in case the deck is fenced.)
10:00 AM
Central Park on foot📍 Directions~2.5 mi
From the arch, north-east past the Carousel and the edge of Sheep Meadow to the foot of the Mall, the elm-lined Literary Walk, which ends at Bethesda Terrace: down the stairs, under the arcade's tiled ceiling, out to the Angel of the Waters fountain and the Lake. West along the water to Bow Bridge — the park's other great bridge — then back past Bethesda and east to Conservatory Water and the Alice in Wonderland statue at 74th. Turn south down the East Drive past the Zoo to the Pond and the stone Gapstow Bridge — the classic view of the skyline and your own hotel — and out at Fifth and 60th. Two-plus hours at an easy pace; paved the whole way.

The Met — the Christmas tree and Neapolitan crèche$30 each
Every December the Met raises a twenty-foot blue spruce in the Medieval Sculpture Hall, hung with eighteenth-century Neapolitan angels and cherubs over a full Baroque nativity, in front of the great iron choir screen from Valladolid — the most beautiful Christmas tree in the city (the 2026 dates aren't posted yet, but it has gone up before Thanksgiving and stayed into January). 5 Av/59 St (N and R; the W weekdays only) is the closest — stairs at the corners of Fifth and 60th, plus part-time stairs on Central Park South by Grand Army Plaza; no elevators. 57 St at Sixth Avenue has the F on weekends and the M on weekdays, with a street elevator at the SW corner of 56th and Sixth. 57 St–7 Av adds the Q.
Paying
OMNY — tap a card, phone or watch
Every subway gate and bus reader takes Apple Pay or a contactless card. The fare is $3.00 a ride in 2026, with free transfers within two hours. Each of you taps your own phone (one card per rider). In Wallet, set a card as the Express Transit card and it works without Face ID. MetroCards are gone.
Rides
Cars, and the congestion charge
The hotel sits at 59th, inside the Manhattan congestion zone, so every Uber or Lyft adds $1.50 (a yellow cab $0.75). Thursday Dec 10 and Friday Dec 11 are city Gridlock Alert Days — Midtown traffic crawls, and walking is faster for anything under a mile.
